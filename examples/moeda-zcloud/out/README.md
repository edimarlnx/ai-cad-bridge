## Zero convention

- **XY zero: the centre of the cavity.** Both files of a block share it.
- **Z zero: the top face of the block** (the flat parting face).
- Safe height 5 mm, clearance 8 mm, both above Z0.
- Deepest legal cut: Z = -2.90 mm (cavity floor -1.80 + relief 1.10). Every posted file was checked against Z >= -3.10.

## Run order

1. Fixture the block, find the cavity centre, set **X0 Y0** there.
2. Touch off the **top face** of the block: **Z0**.
3. Run `<face>-<value>-op1-T1-endmill3175.gcode` (T1 clears the Ø d cavity to Z-1.80).
4. Change to the V-bit. **Do not touch X/Y.** Re-zero **Z only** on the same top face.
5. Run `<face>-<value>-op2_4-T2-vbit30.gcode` (relief, pearls, serration).
6. Deburr, blow out the chips, apply release agent before casting.

Posted with the `grbl` post processor, arguments `--translate_drill` (GRBL has no canned cycles, so the drilling cycle is expanded to G0/G1).

## Honest notes

- **Pearls.** The master models them as Ø1.1 mm beads. A 30° V-bit plunged 0.55 mm leaves a cone about Ø0.30 mm wide at the floor, so the cast pearls are smaller dots than the SLA master's. Going to Ø1.1 mm with this bit would mean plunging 2.05 mm, well past the relief floor. A Ø1 mm ball nose is the fix if the dots read too fine (moeda-zcloud.md already suggests it).
- **Serration.** Modelled as 130 radial notches on the cavity floor at the wall, 0.5 mm deep, not as grooves in the vertical wall: a 2.5-axis machine cannot cut a vertical wall from above. This is the simplification the design note allows.
- **Relief depth.** V-carving is width-driven: a stroke only reaches the full 1.1 mm when it is at least ~0.69 mm wide. Thin serifs come out shallower, which is what gives V-carved lettering its draft. The op is clamped at Z-2.90.
- **The STEP/STL** show the block, the cavity and the pearl dimples. The relief and the serration exist as toolpaths only — their machined shape is defined by the V-bit, not by a modelled pocket.
